## 2. Brand idea

Checkmark Audio helps artists turn an idea into a finished record through skilled engineering, thoughtful collaboration, and a studio environment built around the artist's vision.

The brand should communicate:

> High-end sound, carefully made, in a studio where artists feel heard.

## 3. Brand character

### Core traits

- Refined
- Cinematic
- Warm
- Artist-first
- Confident
- Crafted
- Human
- Community-rooted
- Professional
- Trustworthy

### Desired balance

- Premium polish + authentic studio culture
- Editorial sophistication + practical clarity
- Expertise + collaboration
- Deep blacks + visible warm light
- Strong brand presence + artist-centered storytelling
- Modern quality + local community credibility

### The brand is not

- Cold, elitist, or intimidating
- Generic luxury
- Corporate technology
- A neon nightclub
- A loud streetwear brand
- Busy, gimmicky, or trend-chasing
- School-first; the school remains a separate brand and website

## 4. Audience feeling

An artist encountering Checkmark Audio should feel:

- My work will be taken seriously.
- The team will listen to my vision.
- The studio has professional standards.
- I can belong here without already being famous.
- The process will be clear and collaborative.
- The result will sound finished, intentional, and competitive.

## 5. Color system

The approved color character is deep black, warm cream, refined champagne gold, and a very small red accent. Reference imagery remains the visual authority for subtle warmth and balance.

| Token | Working value | Primary use |
|---|---:|---|
| Studio Black | `#080909` | Primary dark background |
| Soft Black | `#11110F` | Secondary dark surfaces and depth |
| Champagne Gold | `#D2AA70` | Display type, primary actions, premium brand accents |
| Bright Champagne | `#DDB978` | Icons, stars, outlines, dividers, focus details |
| Warm Cream | `#F0E1CF` | Light panels, stationery fields, social proof |
| Cream Highlight | `#F7ECDE` | Light background variation and subtle highlights |
| Ink | `#171511` | Text on cream or gold |
| Accent Red | `#D62E24` | Tiny punctuation, arrows, eyebrow labels, priority moments |

### Approximate visual distribution

- Dark neutrals: 47–60%
- Light neutrals: 30–35%
- Champagne-gold family: 10–18%
- Red: less than 2%; a micro-accent, never a competing brand field

### Color rules

- Gold must read as muted champagne, warm brass, or refined honey.
- Gold must not become lemon yellow, neon, orange, or glittery metallic clip art.
- The brightest gold should be reserved for high-value focal points and thin details.
- Warm cream should feel tactile and editorial, not sterile white or yellow beige.
- Deep blacks must preserve visible detail; do not crush shadows into unreadable darkness.
- Red connects small moments and establishes urgency. It should never dominate the composition.
- Do not use a large red button fill in the approved current direction.
- Verify text contrast for every digital use; adjust lightness while preserving hue character.

## 6. Typography system

The visual typography direction is approved. Final production fonts must be selected by matching the approved characteristics, confirming licensing, and testing web performance and accessibility.

### A. Brand display typography

Used for prominent `CHECKMARK AUDIO` treatments, campaigns, covers, section openings, and high-impact statements.

Characteristics:

- High-contrast modern editorial Roman serif
- Thin hairlines and graceful curves
- Tall, elegant proportions
- Restrained expressive details
- Luxurious but not ornate
- Strong enough to become the largest element without feeling heavy

Candidates to test:

- Cormorant Garamond Light
- Bodoni Moda
- Instrument Serif
- DM Serif Display
- Canela or Editorial New if appropriately licensed

Do not substitute a generic Times-style serif without visual matching.

### B. Expressive italic typography

Used for taglines, select pull quotes, artistic statements, and restrained emotional emphasis.

Characteristics:

- Italic Roman serif
- Curved and graceful
- Expressive without becoming literal cursive
- Legible at display and subheading sizes

Approved example:

`YOUR SOUND, FULLY REALIZED.`

### C. Interface and label typography

Used for navigation, buttons, service labels, names, roles, captions, metadata, and small headings.

Characteristics:

- Tall condensed sans serif
- Uppercase where appropriate
- Moderate tracking
- Clear, architectural, and direct
- Strong legibility at small sizes

Candidates to test:

- Oswald
- Barlow Condensed
- Archivo Narrow
- Bebas Neue for short display labels only

### D. Editorial reading typography

Used for testimonials, longer quotes, selected introductory copy, and premium editorial passages.

Characteristics:

- Warm Roman serif
- Human and expressive
- Moderate contrast
- Generous line height
- Comfortable reading width

### E. Body copy

Use a neutral, highly legible sans serif for practical information, forms, policies, service details, and accessibility-sensitive content.

Candidates:

- Inter
- Source Sans 3
- Manrope

### Typography rules

- Use distinct type roles; do not use one family for everything.
- Keep brand display type elegant rather than chunky or ultra-bold.
- Do not make every heading a condensed sans serif.
- Do not use wedding-style calligraphy or literal cursive as the principal identity font.
- Use short readable lines and generous leading.
- Avoid tiny body copy and excessive uppercase paragraphs.
- Final font selections must include documented licenses and digital fallbacks.

## 8. Brand voice

### Voice principles

- Artist-first: center the artist's goal, not the studio's ego.
- Clear: make the process and next action obvious.
- Confident: state value directly without inflated claims.
- Warm: sound collaborative and welcoming.
- Crafted: choose concise, intentional language.
- Credible: use real work, verified reviews, and confirmed facts.

### Preferred language

- `YOUR SOUND, FULLY REALIZED.`
- `COME WITH AN IDEA. LEAVE WITH A RECORD.`
- `CAPTURE IT RIGHT.`
- `FROM FIRST TAKE TO FINAL MASTER.`
- `YOUR VISION. OUR CRAFT.`
- `IN THEIR OWN WORDS.`
- `WHY ARTISTS COME BACK.`

Use these across different applications. Do not stack every phrase into one page or advertisement.

### Writing style

- Lead with a strong short statement.
- Prefer concrete services and outcomes over abstract hype.
- Keep supporting text brief.
- Let real imagery, music, credits, and reviews prove quality.
- Use direct calls to action such as `BOOK A FREE CONSULTATION` and `HEAR OUR WORK`.

### Avoid in copy

- Generic filler paragraphs
- Unsupported superlatives such as “the best” or “number one”
- Invented awards, credits, statistics, reviews, or client identities
- Overly technical language when speaking to new artists
- Corporate jargon
- Forced slang or imitation of artists' voices
- Repetitive SEO phrasing

## 9. Photography and imagery

### Use

- Real Checkmark Audio studio photography whenever permission is confirmed
- Artists actively singing, playing, producing, or collaborating
- Close and medium compositions with a clear human subject
- Professional microphones, headphones, consoles, instruments, and room details
- Natural concentration, performance, emotion, and interaction
- Warm cinematic grading with champagne highlights and deep black shadows
- Diverse artists shown with authenticity and dignity
- Community evidence through real sessions, reviews, credits, and studio activity

### Lighting

- Warm practical lamps and amber points of light
- Visible facial and rim light
- Warm highlights on microphone grilles and studio hardware
- Shadow detail in dark clothing and room surfaces
- Nighttime or intimate studio mood without dimness

### Avoid

- Generic stock musicians
- Empty luxury interiors as the primary story
- Engineers' backs dominating the main image
- Artificial smiles and corporate poses
- Distorted instruments or impossible microphone setups
- Over-smoothed AI skin
- Neon blue, purple, or green lighting as a dominant brand treatment
- Busy montages and crowded collages
- Unapproved client likenesses or work

### AI imagery rule

AI-generated images may be used for clearly labeled internal concept development. Do not present generated people as real Checkmark Audio clients, testimonials, staff, or documentary studio evidence.

## 10. Graphic language

- Use strong editorial scale contrasts.
- Favor flat black and warm-cream fields with precise champagne details.
- Use thin rules, fine-line icons, disciplined grids, and generous negative space.
- Keep corner radii minimal and refined rather than soft SaaS-style pills.
- Use texture sparingly: subtle paper grain or warm photographic texture is acceptable.
- Keep red to tiny purposeful connections.

Avoid glassmorphism, heavy shadows, sparkly gold, excessive gradients, cartoon icons, and decorative clutter.

## 11. Buttons and calls to action

### Primary action

- Solid champagne-gold background
- Near-black condensed uppercase label
- Minimal corner radius
- Clear, prominent, and easy to find
- Digital hover may lift slightly or brighten within the same champagne family

### Secondary action

- Black or transparent background
- Bright-champagne border and label
- A tiny red arrow may provide the accent connection
- Must remain legible without turning bright yellow

### CTA hierarchy

- Use one dominant action per view.
- Keep booking, calling, texting, and listening easy to locate.
- Do not create multiple equally loud buttons.
- Do not use bouncing, flashing, or pulsing CTAs.

## 12. Reviews and social proof

- Use only verified reviews with approved attribution.
- Never invent names, roles, ratings, or quotations.
- Keep five-star displays attached to their individual verified reviews.
- Do not add decorative ratings that could imply a separate aggregate score.
- Prioritize reviews, music samples, studio imagery, and real credits as authenticity signals.
- Current preferred digital treatment: three equal, photo-free review columns on warm cream with expressive serif quotations.

## 13. Application guidance

### Website

- Editorial display title, singer-focused imagery, visible booking action, and proof near the top
- Alternating dark and light sections may be used when the sequence remains cohesive
- No autoplay audio
- Essential copy must remain real text, not embedded only in images

### Social media

- Use one strong statement, one real image, and one clear action
- Preserve generous margins and strong contrast
- Avoid filling every area with text, logos, badges, or effects

### Print and proposals

- Lead with the editorial serif and disciplined grid
- Use warm cream as a paper-like field and black for structure
- Use gold selectively; verify print reproduction and foil/metallic specifications separately
- Use red only for small navigation or priority markers

### Apparel and merchandise

- Use only the canonical logo files listed in Section 7
- Prefer one-color or limited-color applications
- Test embroidery and small-size reproduction separately

## 14. Accessibility and integrity

- Meet WCAG AA contrast for normal text and controls.
- Provide visible keyboard focus in digital products.
- Never use red alone to communicate meaning.
- Provide descriptive alt text based on page context.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Secure permission for identifiable people, client work, music, quotes, credits, and third-party marks.
- Do not autoplay sound.

## 15. Keep / omit summary

### Keep

- Editorial high-contrast serif identity typography
- Expressive italic Roman accents
- Condensed uppercase interface labels
- Deep black, warm cream, champagne gold, and tiny red accents
- Warm cinematic artist photography
- Artist-first and community-rooted storytelling
- Short, confident copy
- Real music, verified reviews, and real studio evidence
- Clean grids and generous negative space

### Omit

- The generated mockup logo
- Any unapproved logo candidate
- Bright lemon-yellow gold
- Large red fields and red button fills
- Literal cursive identity fonts
- Chunky oversized sans-serif branding
- Neon nightclub palettes
- Generic luxury or SaaS styling
- Busy collages and excessive effects
- Invented testimonials, clients, credits, or claims
- Wordy descriptive subtext when the label is already clear
